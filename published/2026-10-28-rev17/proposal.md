# Autumn Budget 2026: the policies explained

**What the Budget did with the 20 measures on our list, the ones it added, and what each costs**

A PolicyEngine briefing · after the Budget of 28 October 2026 · October 2026

PolicyEngine figures are from the Budget dashboard at [policyengine.org/uk/budgets/autumn-2026](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/budgets/autumn-2026), manifest revision 17 (2026-10-28 19:24 UTC).

Run together, the seven measures in the dashboard cost £3,110 million in 2026-27 and £615 million in 2027-28, and raise £210 million in 2028-29, when the property income rise outweighs the two tax cuts still in force. The combined run is signed off.

## How to read this

The day before the Budget we circulated 20 measures most likely to come up. This version says what the Budget actually did with each, and adds the measures it made that the list missed. For every measure:

- **What changes**, and when, from the Budget documents.
- **Who it affects**, using the government's own reach figures where it gives them.
- **The official costing**: the government's costing, certified by the Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR), from the OBR's [detailed forecast table 1.1](https://obr.uk/efo/economic-and-fiscal-outlook-october-2026/). For a measure in our dashboard we compare with the line or lines of that table that match what we model, and give the total in the Treasury's Table 6.1, which rounds to the nearest £5 million and includes the measure's other lines, for context only. Fuel duty is the exception: its official figure is the government's estimate, which the OBR used but could not certify. For other measures we give the OBR's total for the measure. In every table a negative number is a cost to the public finances and a positive number raises money, in £ million.
- **Our estimate**, only where the dashboard publishes one, with the reasons it differs from the official costing.
- **How we model it**, or "not modelled".

Our estimates differ from the official costings for four main reasons: timing (we score each tax year's liability; the official costings are on a National Accounts basis, in which tax paid through Self Assessment can fall in a later year), coverage (we use household survey data, so we estimate less fully the effects outside households and on incomes the survey records thinly), behaviour (ours are static; most official costings include responses) and scope (the parts of a policy each models).

### Fuel duty: the 5p cut kept to July 2027

[Announced in a different form]{.badge .changed} [Our list: no. 1, "Fuel duty freeze", odds 93%]{.prev}

|  |  |
|:------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What changes** | The 5p cut, which holds the main rate at 52.95p a litre, now runs to 1 July 2027 instead of ending in two steps in January and March 2027, and the RPI rise planned for April 2027 moves to the same day. On 1 July 2027 rates rise by 5p and by RPI; from 1 April 2028 they rise by RPI each April. We had expected the freeze to run to March 2028. |
| **Who it affects** | 35 million motorists and businesses across the UK, the Budget says. |
| **How we model it** | The main rate stays at 52.95p a litre to 30 June 2027 and returns to 60.59p from 1 July 2027. Rates are weighted by days into fiscal years, and the VAT on the lower pump price is included. Static: households buy the same fuel. |
| **Read more** | [The fuel duty cut extension in the Autumn Budget 2026](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/research/fuel-duty-cut-extension) |

| £ million | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|:--------------------------|------:|------:|------:|------:|------:|------:|
| Official: Fuel Duty | −574 | −782 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Table 6.1 total | −575 | −780 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| PolicyEngine | −301 | −521 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

[Why ours differs]{.why} The official figure is the government's estimate, which the OBR used but could not certify. Ours is £273 million lower in 2026-27 and £261 million lower in 2027-28. Most of the gap is coverage: we count only the petrol and diesel households buy, about £13 billion of the £24 billion of fuel duty the OBR forecasts for 2026-27, while the official costing covers all taxable fuel, including fuel businesses buy. Scaled up to the OBR's fuel duty receipts, our fuel duty cost would be about £450 million in 2026-27 and £760 million in 2027-28. VAT and behaviour narrow the gap: ours includes £50 million and £87 million of VAT on the lower pump price, which the official fuel duty figure leaves out, and the official costing's demand response makes its cost smaller. The rest of the 2026-27 gap fits the official costing if it assumes the full 5p returns on 1 January 2027 (about £555 million on 45 billion litres), where the law brings it back as 3p then and 2p more from 1 March (about £410 million).

### Pension Tax Allowance

[Announced in a different form]{.badge .changed} [Our list: no. 3, "State Pension tax protection", odds 90%]{.prev}

|  |  |
|:------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What changes** | From 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2030, people over State Pension age, or who reach it during the tax year, get an income tax reduction. Those whose total income from all sources is no more than the full new State Pension get it in full: in 2027-28, 20% of £466.40 (£13,036.40 − £12,570) is £93.28, rounded up to £94. Above that it falls by £1 for every £3 of income and is gone at £13,319. We had expected a full allowance with a cliff edge; the Budget tapers it instead. Scottish taxpayers get it at 20%. |
| **Who it affects** | In 2027-28, around 290,000 pensioners pay no tax because of it and around 500,000 get a reduction; around 1 million by the end of the Parliament (Treasury explainer). |
| **How we model it** | A non-refundable reduction in income tax from 2027-28 to 2029-30, worth up to £94 in 2027-28 and withdrawn at £1 for every £3 of total income above the full new State Pension. Total income includes savings interest. The personal allowance is unchanged. We count people as over State Pension age if they reach it by 6 October, so people who reach it later in the tax year are left out. |

### Carer's Allowance: a taper above the earnings limit

[Announced]{.badge .announced} [Our list: no. 2, odds 92%]{.prev}

|  |  |
|:------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What changes** | Carers no longer lose all of Carer's Allowance when they earn more than the Weekly Earnings Limit. Above the limit, set at 16 times the National Living Wage (16 × £13.21 = £211.36 a week at April 2027's rate), the allowance is withdrawn £1 for each £1 of earnings, a 100% taper. It applies in England and Wales and starts during 2030-31, once a new IT system is in place. |
| **Who it affects** | Around 48,000 carers in England and Wales, the Budget says. |
| **How we model it** | In England and Wales, carers whose weekly net earnings exceed the limit, taken as 16 hours at the National Living Wage rounded up to the pound (£212 a week at April 2027's rate), keep the allowance less £1 for each £1 of earnings above the limit. We count half of 2030-31. Static: no change in how much carers work. One limitation: for a carer on Universal Credit, the model counts the extra allowance in full as unearned income but deducts none of the income tax on it from earnings, so such a carer can lose overall. |
| **Read more** | [The Carer's Allowance earnings taper in the Autumn Budget 2026](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/research/carers-allowance-taper) |

| £ million | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|:--------------------------|------:|------:|------:|------:|------:|------:|
| Official: Welfare inside cap | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | −33 | −69 |
| Table 6.1 total | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | −40 | −80 |
| PolicyEngine | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | −24 | −48 |

Table 6.1's total also includes the measure's other line in the OBR's table: Scottish BGA (current).

[Why ours differs]{.why} The measure's total in the OBR's table is £38 million in 2030-31 and £79 million in 2031-32, before the Treasury rounds it to the nearest £5 million. Its Carer's Allowance and Universal Credit spending, the Welfare inside cap line, is £33 million and £69 million. Ours, £24 million and £48 million, is net of the income tax on the higher allowance (£15 million and £32 million); before that tax, ours is £39 million and £79 million, about a sixth more than the OBR's spending.

### Trading allowance: £1,000 to £3,000

[New]{.badge .new} [Not on our list]{.prev}

|  |  |
|:------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What changes** | From 6 April 2027 the trading allowance triples from £1,000 to £3,000, so up to £3,000 of trading and miscellaneous income a year is tax-free. |
| **Who it affects** | People with small amounts of trading or miscellaneous income. The Budget presents it as support for entrepreneurs taking the first steps in starting a small business. |
| **How we model it** | The allowance rises to £3,000 from 2027-28 for income tax and Class 4 National Insurance. Without gross receipts in the data, the model relieves a profit up to £3,000 in full and gives no relief above it, so the cost may be overstated for some people and understated for others. |
| **Read more** | [Autumn Budget 2026: raising the trading allowance](https://www.policyengine.org/uk/research/uk-autumn-budget-2026-trading-allowance) |

| £ million | 2026-27 | 2027-28 | 2028-29 | 2029-30 | 2030-31 | 2031-32 |
|:--------------------------|------:|------:|------:|------:|------:|------:|
| Official: Income tax and NICs | 0 | 0 | −221 | −165 | −171 | −178 |
| Table 6.1 total | 0 | 0 | −225 | −165 | −175 | −180 |
| PolicyEngine | 0 | −110 | −118 | −119 | −120 | −130 |

Table 6.1's total also includes the measure's other lines in the OBR's table: PSCE in RDEL, Scottish BGA (current) and Scottish self-financed expenditure.

[Why ours differs]{.why} From 2028-29 ours falls £46 million to £103 million a year below the OBR's income tax and NICs lines (27% to 47%), with the largest gap in 2028-29. The official costing includes people switching from deducting their actual expenses to the larger allowance, which we don't model. Without gross receipts in the survey data, we give traders with profits above £3,000 nothing from the change and give miscellaneous income no allowance at all, while for some profits between £1,000 and £3,000 we overstate the gain, because we leave them unrelieved under the current £1,000 allowance. In 2027-28 the OBR's lines have no cost and ours has £110 million. The dashboard gives the reason in its description of the comparison: the official costings are on a National Accounts basis, in which tax paid through Self Assessment can fall in a later year, so a change such as the trading allowance can appear about a year later in them than in ours.

## What we're improving

We plan to update these results later this week. The update will:

- add poverty and people-affected figures;
- add national figures for the savings income tax rise, once we have improved our savings income data (the calculator already includes it);
- apply the trading allowance to miscellaneous income as well as trading income, which will raise our estimate of its cost;
- correct how the Carer's Allowance taper interacts with overlapping benefits, such as the State Pension, in 2030-31;
- extend the fuel duty cut to road fuel gases (LPG and natural gas), as the government's costing does;
- give the Pension Tax Allowance to everyone who reaches State Pension age during the tax year.

We are also working on:

- projecting household electricity spending with retail energy prices rather than general inflation;
- adding the Budget's measures to PolicyEngine UK itself, so anyone using our open-source model can run them.
